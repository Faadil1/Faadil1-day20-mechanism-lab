# Day 20 — Evidence Ledger

## Strongly supported working observations

- Observable behavior is not equivalent to stated or endorsed intention.
- Recommendation systems act on proxies and inferred preferences rather than direct access to intention.
- Some empirical work shows a tension between immediate engagement/enjoyment and reflectively endorsed or ideal preferences.
- Users can experience limited causal legibility around how actions affect recommendation environments.
- Hypertext, search, feeds, and answer engines expose different degrees of route, provenance, and transition visibility.
- Link rot, zero-click behavior, personalized ranking, and AI answer surfaces show that retraceability and source traversal can materially change across architectures.

## Preserved counter-cases

- Human-directed or chronological systems are not automatically more diverse, democratic, or satisfying.
- Algorithmic systems can increase discovery, support, relevance, and serendipity.
- Early-web browsing was often disorienting and heavily mediated by portals, directories, search, and gatekeepers.
- More control does not guarantee that people will use controls.
- Platform effects vary; no universal polarization or radicalization claim is permitted.

## Claims killed as foundations

- old web was simply better
- serendipity died
- algorithms always narrow users
- chronological feed fixes agency
- filter bubbles are the universal harm
- human curation is neutral
- visible links alone restore agency
