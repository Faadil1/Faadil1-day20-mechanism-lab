## Why relevant

- how objects were passed from person to person;
- why someone clicked, saved, returned, or shared;
- hyperlinks, fandoms, profiles, animations, and personal sites as social rituals;
- where durable traces survived or disappeared;
- competing recollections and negative experiences of earlier systems.

## How this should affect Sediment Atlas / The Buried Path if that direction survives its gate

Use independently verifiable cultural objects as reasons to explore or revisit. A real object should have:
1. artifact ID and source provenance;
2. author / original distribution context when verifiable;
3. navigable relationships to other artifacts;
4. at least one purposeful interaction the user can take (read context, compare, save, follow a credited connection, revisit);
5. an inspectable observation receipt rather than a claim about internal motivation;
6. rights/permission status for reproduction, thumbnails, audiovisual assets, and quotations.

A historic animation shared through AIM or a personal homepage is **a candidate content example**, not evidence that early internet had universal agency or superior well-being.
